### Rule 1: Visibility Principle (Red Line)

1. Core principle: **If it is not visible, it is not written**. Characters/elements/scenes that are not in the picture are directly ignored.

2. Restrictions on shot size description:

- Level shot/close-up of the upper body: do not describe the lower body; close-up of the head: do not describe below the neck; close-up of the chest and above: do not describe below the chest.

3. Action description: Break down and combine the actions, and write **frozen-frame action details** (not simple actions, such as "the body is tilted and floating in the air, with arms and legs outstretched, face down, and a surprised expression"), without process description.

4. Physical state: No omissions (e.g., lying in bed/eyes closed); no intentional/speculatory/transitional content (disable attempts/will/because...so/although...but).

2. Based on the principle of visibility, **different elements are selected for different perspectives** (e.g., the ceiling is not shown from a high perspective, and the floor is not shown from a low perspective).

3. The scene content in the picture should be reasonable. For example, if the picture shows a person entering a room from the outside, then the background should be the scene outside the door.

4. Describe elements and details objectively, maintaining consistency with the scene. Descriptions of smells, voices, sounds, atmosphere, and actions are prohibited, as are metaphors and exaggerations.
